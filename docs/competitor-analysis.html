UK Competitor Analysis

Peer-to-peer driveway and private parking marketplaces: what they offer, where they fall short, and where ParkSpace can win.

Prepared for stakeholders · 2 October 2026 · Working name: ParkSpace (placeholder brand) · Launch market: United Kingdom (GBP)

1. Executive summary

  • The market is led by two well-reviewed marketplaces, JustPark and YourParkingSpace, with Parklet focused on longer lets. Both leaders have strong driver ratings (about 4.5 out of 5 on Trustpilot), so the opening is not basic booking. It is the pain points around the booking.
  • The most repeated complaints are about reliability and recourse: hosts cancelling or deactivating listings at short notice, drivers arriving to find the space taken, refunds that are hard to get, and slow support.
  • ParkSpace's differentiators (all already built in the working proof of concept): an instant full refund when a space is blocked, price negotiation, paid extras (CCTV, EV charging matched to the driver's connector), request-to-book with a card hold, a structured overstay flow, and a disputes desk with payout freezing. None of these were visible on the competitor pages reviewed.
  • The biggest commercial risk is price to hosts. ParkSpace's planned 20% host commission is far above what JustPark reportedly charges hosts (about 3%) and what YourParkingSpace charges hosts (3%, with its own 20% uplift added to the driver's price instead). On identical pricing, hosts would earn less with ParkSpace.
  • Recommendation: launch in one dense area, lead with reliability ("guaranteed space or full refund"), and decide the commission model before launch (see section 7).

2. Scope and method

Desk research on 2 October 2026 using public web pages: the competitors' own host and driver pages, Trustpilot review pages, UK money-advice sites, and driver-survey write-ups. Fee figures marked "third-party" come from write-ups rather than the company's own terms and should be confirmed against current terms before external use. "Not seen" means the feature was not on the pages read; it does not prove the feature is absent. Adjacent players (section 5) are summarised from general knowledge plus survey results and were not researched in depth.

3. Direct competitors

3.1 JustPark: the market leader

What they offer

  • Free to list; hourly, daily or monthly rentals; date blocking and set hours
  • Manual or automated pricing, including scheduled peak pricing
  • Automatic payouts; mobile app for remote management
  • Driver verification by number plate; optional property-damage protection
  • Polished driver app with reminders and reviews; roughly 4.5 stars on Trustpilot
  • Full refund when the driver cancels more than 24 hours ahead; the transaction fee is refunded if the owner or JustPark cancels

Gaps and complaints

  • Host cancellations: reviews describe owners deactivating listings the night before, with JustPark calling it outside its control
  • Space occupied: arriving to find the space taken, with no refund for extra costs
  • Refunds: some customers say they were hard to obtain even when entitled
  • Liability: the driver agreement puts responsibility for the vehicle on the driver and caps JustPark's liability at the greater of the amount paid or £100
  • Not seen on host pages: price negotiation, paid extras, request-to-book, a host-side cancellation policy

Fees: hosts pay a small service fee. A third-party guide puts it at about 3%, higher on a first long booking, plus a 50p fee on small withdrawals (unconfirmed on JustPark's own pages). Drivers pay a transaction fee; its size is not stated in the driver agreement.

3.2 YourParkingSpace

What they offer

  • Highest-rated UK parking app in one survey (4.6/5); Trustpilot about 4.5 across roughly 110,000 reviews
  • Wide supply: driveways, garages, commercial and residential car parks, even empty land
  • Free to list; hosts keep 100% of their own price less a 3% processing fee
  • The driver sees the host's price plus a 20% uplift, which funds marketing and growth

Gaps and complaints

  • Support speed: one reviewer reports almost four weeks to resolve a parking issue
  • Booking friction: owners not responding, bookings declined, confirmation problems
  • Refunds: a reviewer had refund requests rejected twice and was quoted £0 for a cancellation under 24 hours
  • Arrival: unclear signage and wrong directions to the exact spot
  • Not seen: negotiation, paid extras, an overstay prompt to the host

3.3 Parklet

What they offer

  • Established in 2004; 20,000+ spaces; used by councils and national car-park operators
  • Strong in monthly and weekly lets, including residential bays
  • Fees: 20% + VAT (monthly, plus a £37.50 + VAT first-month admin fee), 25% + VAT (1–12 weeks), 30% + VAT (1–6 days)

Gaps

  • High commission, especially on short lets
  • Built for long contracts, not "park for two hours now"
  • Older, listing-style product rather than an app-first, on-demand experience

7. Opportunities and recommendations

Where ParkSpace can differentiate

  • "Space guaranteed or full refund." Directly answers the top complaint against both leaders. The mechanism already exists in the product.
  • Negotiation and extras create value for both sides (hosts fill quiet hours; drivers get better prices and add-ons) and are not visible at the leaders.
  • Faster, clearer resolution. Support speed is a recurring complaint; the admin disputes tools, payout freezing and audit trail are a ready-made operating model.
  • On-demand short stays where Parklet is weakest.

Decisions needed before launch

  • Commission model. Options: (a) keep 20% from hosts, (b) charge hosts a low fee and add a visible driver service fee, or (c) a blended model with a lower launch rate for early hosts. The platform's commission setting is already editable by admin.
  • Liability and insurance. Competitors disclaim vehicle liability or offer optional damage protection. Obtain legal advice and decide on an insurance partner.
  • Launch area. Network density decides this market. Pick one city or borough and recruit host supply there first.
  • Payments and notifications. Move from simulated to Stripe Connect and real email / SMS / push before any public pilot.

8. Risks

  • Incumbent network effects: JustPark and YourParkingSpace already hold the supply and the brand trust of tens of thousands of reviews.
  • Host economics: a 20% cut may make recruiting hosts expensive (see section 4).
  • Trust and safety: blocked-space and host-cancellation problems appear across the sector; reliability must be proven in practice, not just in the product design.
  • Data confidence: fee figures marked third-party should be verified before they appear in investor or partner material.
